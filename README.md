# NSUN2_m5C
Stem cell exhaustion is a hallmark of aging, yet the role of epitranscriptomic regulation in this process remains poorly understood. Among various RNA modifications, 5-methylcytosine (m5C) has been implicated in mRNA metabolism, but its functional impact on stem cell homeostasis and senescence is largely unknown.By profiling the m5C epitranscriptome across human embryonic, neural, and mesenchymal stem cells (hESCs, hNSCs, and hMSCs), we identified NSUN2 as a key regulator of m5C deposition required for hMSC function. NSUN2 loss provoked premature senescence, proliferative decline, and impaired differentiation in hMSCs. Mechanistically, NSUN2 stabilized development- and proliferation-related transcripts, and its catalytic activity was indispensable for counteracting cellular senescence. Senescent phenotypes were alleviated by reconstituting wild-type NSUN2, but not a catalytically inactive mutant, and were mimicked by pharmacological NSUN2 inhibition. Depletion of the m5C reader YBX1 preferentially destabilized modified transcripts and induced congruent aging phenotypes, delineating a critical NSUN2–m5C–YBX1 axis in safeguarding human stem cell homeostasis.

Our study supports a role for m5C RNA methylation in hMSC homeostasis and suggests a contributory role for reduced NSUN2–m5C–YBX1 activity in cellular senescence. These findings provide insight into epitranscriptomic regulation of stem-cell fate and motivate further evaluation of the NSUN2–m5C pathway as a potential intervention target.
